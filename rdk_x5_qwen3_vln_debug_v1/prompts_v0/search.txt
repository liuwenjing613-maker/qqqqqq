1. VISIBLE LOCAL WAYPOINT TEST

First ask:

Can the next useful local motion be represented by one concrete visible and reachable floor point in the current image?

A valid POINT must:
- lie on real visible floor;
- be connected to the robot-side floor without crossing an obstacle;
- represent a direction that the robot can follow now;
- lie inside a usable floor region, not on a wall or obstacle boundary.

The POINT is only for the next local motion and the next observation.
It does not need to represent the complete route.

Therefore use POINT when:
- broad reachable floor remains before a distant wall, partition, obstacle, or later corner;
- a left or right passage is already visible;
- a local obstacle blocks the centre but a visible reachable bypass exists on either side;
- the route will turn later, but the robot can still move toward a visible floor waypoint now.

Use the POINT coordinate itself to steer left or right.

2. TURN TEST

Consider TURN_LEFT or TURN_RIGHT only if Step 1 fails.

Use TURN only when:
- no concrete visible reachable floor point can represent the next useful motion;
- the current visible floor has ended at a blocking wall, partition, furniture group, or obstacle;
- the required continuation must first be revealed by rotating the camera view.

Do not output TURN merely because:
- a wall or corner is visible farther ahead;
- the route will eventually turn;
- one side appears wider or more open;
- the best waypoint is uncertain.

If a side passage or bypass floor is already visible, use POINT on that floor instead of TURN.

3. COMPETITION-SCENE RULES

- Gray low partitions are solid non-traversable boundaries. Floor visible behind or above them is not reachable.
- Spaces under blue chairs or through chair legs are not passages.
- Narrow gaps between partitions or furniture are not passages unless they clearly form a normal usable floor route.
- A broom or dustpan is a local obstacle. If a visible floor bypass exists around it, use POINT on the bypass.
- Do not place p on the front or side face of a partition, cabinet, table, chair, broom, dustpan, or other obstacle.

Use these contrast cases:

- Wide floor before a distant partition or later corner: POINT.
- A central broom or dustpan with a visible side bypass: POINT on the bypass.
- A side corridor already visible in the image: POINT inside that corridor.
- A blocking partition directly ends the visible route, only an unusable floor remnant remains before it, and the continuation is outside the image: TURN.
- After a small turn reveals reachable floor: immediately return to POINT.
