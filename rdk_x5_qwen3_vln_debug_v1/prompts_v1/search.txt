- apply the following decision tree exactly

STEP 1 — TEST THREE LOCAL FLOOR REGIONS

Inspect three overlapping regions in the lower-middle part of the image:

- LEFT-FRONT region: approximately x=100 to 450
- FRONT region: approximately x=300 to 700
- RIGHT-FRONT region: approximately x=550 to 900

Use approximately y=450 to 850 as the local waypoint area.

These coordinates define visual search regions only. They do not represent real distance.

A region is VALID when:

- it contains a clear patch of real visible floor;
- that floor is connected to the floor near the bottom centre of the image;
- reaching that floor does not cross a wall, partition, furniture, broom, dustpan, chair, object, or other obstacle;
- the point can be placed clearly inside the floor patch, not on an obstacle or boundary.

The floor patch does not need to continue beyond the next wall, corner, or obstacle.

It only needs to provide one useful local movement before the next observation.

STEP 2 — MANDATORY POINT RULE

If at least one of LEFT-FRONT, FRONT, or RIGHT-FRONT is VALID:

- a must be "POINT"
- TURN_LEFT and TURN_RIGHT are forbidden
- place p inside the best valid floor region

Choose among valid regions in this order:

1. the region with the clearest and largest visible floor patch;
2. the region with fewer visible obstacles;
3. the region closer to the current forward direction.

Use an off-centre POINT when the valid region is on the left or right.

The following cases must use POINT:

- broad floor is visible before a distant wall, partition, cabinet, or corner;
- the route will turn later, but any valid local floor region exists now;
- a local obstacle blocks the centre, but LEFT-FRONT or RIGHT-FRONT is valid;
- a side passage has already entered the image;
- the long-term route is uncertain, but a valid local floor point exists.

STEP 3 — STRICT TURN RULE

TURN is allowed only when BOTH conditions are true:

A. LEFT-FRONT, FRONT, and RIGHT-FRONT are all INVALID.

B. A solid wall, low partition, cabinet, furniture group, or obstacle visibly blocks the central lower-forward area, so the camera must rotate before any useful local floor point can be selected.

If either A or B is false:

- TURN is forbidden
- output POINT

A distant wall, distant corner, distant partition endpoint, or one side looking more open does not satisfy condition B.

Uncertainty does not satisfy condition B.

Knowing that the route will eventually turn does not satisfy condition B.

STEP 4 — TURN DIRECTION

Use this step only after both TURN conditions A and B are satisfied.

Choose TURN_LEFT only when the visible wall ending, corner, or opening supports the left side.

Choose TURN_RIGHT only when the visible wall ending, corner, or opening supports the right side.

Use the more open side only to choose LEFT or RIGHT after TURN has already been proven necessary.

For TURN_LEFT or TURN_RIGHT:

- p=null

STEP 5 — COMPETITION-SCENE REJECTIONS

- Gray low partitions are solid non-traversable boundaries.
- Floor visible behind or above a partition is not reachable.
- Spaces under blue chairs or through chair legs are not valid floor regions.
- Narrow gaps between partitions or furniture are not valid passages.
- A broom or dustpan is a local obstacle.
- If a visible floor region exists beside a broom or dustpan, use POINT on that side.
- Never place p on a wall, partition, cabinet, chair, broom, dustpan, obstacle, or furniture surface.

FINAL CHECK

Before returning TURN, check LEFT-FRONT, FRONT, and RIGHT-FRONT one more time.

If any one of them contains a valid local floor point:

- output POINT

Only if all three regions are invalid and the central lower-forward area is visibly blocked:

- output TURN_LEFT or TURN_RIGHT
